Privacy Policy

Snuggery: Mini-Apps, for iPhone

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Snuggery does not collect any personal data. Nothing you do in it is sent to its developer.

Everything you import into Snuggery — your files, your folders, and the mini-apps you run — stays on your device. Snuggery has no servers, no account system, and no analytics. It is offline by default: it goes online only for a connection you set up yourself, described under Network, and never uploads your files.

Mini-apps

Mini-apps run inside Snuggery are blocked from reaching the internet. They can read only their own files and keep only their own saved data, inside their own sandbox, and cannot see each other's data or the rest of your library.

Purchases

Purchases are handled entirely by Apple. Snuggery receives only whether a purchase was made; it never sees your payment details, and it has no way to identify you.

Network

Offline by default. Until you set up a connection, Snuggery makes no network requests of its own.

Connections you set up. A mini-app's Keep This Up To Date screen can take a web address for that app's data file. Nothing is fetched until you approve the address. The approval shows the site's address and tells you that the site sees each time you open the app. An app can also suggest the address of its own data. Snuggery asks once, the first time you open the app, on the same approval sheet, and fetches nothing unless you tap Allow; Not Now is remembered. After you approve an address, Snuggery fetches from that address, and only that address:

  • when you open the app, when you come back to it, and when you tap Get New Data;
  • in the background, now and then when iOS allows, but only for an app you turned that on for;
  • when a Shortcuts automation of yours runs Refresh a Snuggery App, again only for an app you turned background refresh on for.

Every connection is listed on Snuggery's Connections screen, and you can remove any of them there.

What is sent. An ordinary HTTPS request for the address you gave. The site sees what any website sees: your IP address, the time, and that the request came from Snuggery and which version of it. If you added a key (a GitHub sign-in, or a token you pasted), it is sent only to that site, and only for addresses inside the repository or folder you chose. When you start a job from Snuggery, the site receives only what you set up for that job. Snuggery sends no cookies and nothing from your library. Only a successful answer is saved, and only into that app's data file.

GitHub. Signing in to GitHub is optional. If you do, you sign in on GitHub's own page, and you install Snuggery's GitHub App only on the repositories you choose. Snuggery then contacts github.com and api.github.com to keep the sign-in current, to read the files you connected, to list the repositories you installed Snuggery's GitHub App on, to list the folders and files of a repository you chose when you use Choose from GitHub…, and its workflows when you set up Rebuild Data, and, when you ask it to rebuild an app's data, to start the workflow you chose and follow it until it finishes, then fetch the new data. Snuggery never asks you to enter a code someone else sent you. One sign-in covers every app whose address is inside a repository you installed Snuggery's GitHub App on.

Keys stay on this iPhone. Sign-ins and tokens are kept in the iPhone's Keychain, on this device only. They are not synced to iCloud, not included in Snuggery's backups, and never sent anywhere but the site they belong to. A Snuggery backup carries a connection's name and address without its key, and a restored connection does nothing until you confirm it again, and uses your GitHub sign-in only if you turn that on when you confirm.

Disconnect. Removing a connection, or disconnecting a sign-in, forgets the key on this iPhone at once, whether or not you have bought Snuggery. It cannot cancel the key at GitHub itself, so Snuggery links you to the GitHub page where you can.

Nothing goes to the developer. Every request goes from your iPhone straight to a site you named. Snuggery has no server to send anything to, and its developer never sees your addresses, your keys or your data. One thing GitHub itself records: Snuggery's GitHub App is registered by its developer, and GitHub shows an App's owner which accounts installed it and on which repositories. The developer keeps the App's key offline and never uses it to read anything. iOS's App Privacy Report (Settings → Privacy & Security → App Privacy Report, once you turn it on) lists the sites Snuggery contacted, so you can check them against your connections.

Shortcuts and the examples page. Keep This Up To Date can also open Apple's Shortcuts app on a shortcut you named there. Anything that shortcut fetches, Shortcuts fetches with your own accounts; Snuggery only opens it and gets nothing back, and mini-apps cannot open it at all. The Every example, ready to download row opens the examples page in Safari's own sheet: Safari loads the page, as it would in its own app, and Snuggery sees nothing of it but a ZIP you choose to open in Snuggery.

Notifications. If you turn on Notifications for an app on its Keep This Up To Date screen, Snuggery shows the alerts that app's own data file lists, as notifications on this iPhone. They are local notifications: Snuggery reads them from data already on your iPhone and posts them itself. Nothing is sent anywhere to make them, there is no push server, and the app's own code has no part in it. It is off for every app until you turn it on, and a restored backup turns it off again. Like any app's notifications, they can show on the lock screen and on a paired Apple Watch, with as much of their text as iOS's own notification preview settings allow.

Ask, in the ⋯ menu on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, answers questions about a file, a folder, an app or your whole Library with Apple's on-device model. The text it reads and the answer it gives stay on your iPhone; nothing is sent anywhere, and Snuggery keeps no record of your questions.

Children

Snuggery is suitable for any age. Because it collects no data at all, it collects no data from children either.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the date at the top of this page changes with it. Snuggery collecting nothing is a design decision rather than a current setting, so a change here would mean a change to the product, announced in its release notes.

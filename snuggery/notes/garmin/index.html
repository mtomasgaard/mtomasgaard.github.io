Your Garmin data, as an app on your iPhone

Ask an AI for the dashboard you want. Keep it on your phone.

If you ask an AI about your Garmin data — this week against your plan, last night's sleep, how the long runs are trending — the answers end up in a chat. With Snuggery they can end up on your phone instead: a dashboard of your own that lives in Snuggery, works without a signal, and is hosted by nobody else.

Three steps

  1. Describe it. In Snuggery's Create tab, say what you want in plain words — this week against my plan, training load, heart rate and my long runs. Snuggery turns that into a prompt.
  2. Your AI builds it, round by round. Paste the prompt into the AI you already use with your Garmin data. It sends back a ZIP: the app, with your data inside. Ask for what is missing or wrong, and each new ZIP replaces the last one in place.
  3. Open the ZIP in Snuggery. It runs on your iPhone, in its own sandbox, with no account and no server.

Or start from the one in the video

The dashboard in the video is the Running Dashboard from Snuggery's open template, which an AI built over many rounds of asking; the runner in it is made up. You can open it in your browser, or get the ZIP and open it in Snuggery. To fill it with your own runs, make a private copy of the template repository (it will hold your heart rate, your weight and the track of every run) and give its running-dashboard/PROMPT.md to a coding agent: it walks through connecting your Garmin account, with you typing the password and the code yourself. The written verdicts, like the On track at the top, come from a separate daily AI session you can add later; the data job alone brings the numbers.

Fresh every morning, if you set it up

A dashboard shows the data it was built with. To keep it current, the template's Garmin job can run each morning in your private copy on GitHub, and Snuggery fetches the result when you open the app — a connection you set up and approve yourself. It takes some setting up: a Garmin sign-in from a computer (repeated now and then, when its session runs out), a secret on GitHub, and a clock to start the job, because the job has no schedule of its own; PROMPT.md walks a coding agent through all of it. The job signs in through an open-source library, not an official Garmin API, so a change on Garmin's side can stop it until the library catches up. Snuggery is offline by default and online only where you choose.

The loop tells how my own dashboard was set up, when a Shortcut still had to deliver the file; since 1.3, Snuggery can fetch it itself.
